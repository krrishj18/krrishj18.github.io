About

I build autonomy for teams of drones that have to work where people can't safely go.

I'm a researcher at Carnegie Mellon's Robotics Institute, in the AirLab. My work sits between perception and planning: getting semantic understanding to run onboard a small aerial vehicle, and getting several of those vehicles to divide up a search without a central operator or a reliable radio link.

The problems I care about are the ones that only show up outside the lab — degraded communication, maps that are wrong, teammates that drop out mid-mission. Most of what I do ends up as open-source infrastructure other people can fly.

Research

Multi-robot semantic search

Finding specific targets in unknown environments with a drone team. Semantic detections are fused into a voxel map, triangulated across viewpoints by ray intersection, clustered into target hypotheses, and allocated to robots through a decentralized bid auction.

semantic mappingtriangulationtask allocationsearch & rescue

Lifelong planning in unknown, dynamic spaces

Hybrid centralized–decentralized multi-agent path finding for non-holonomic robots, combining PIBT with Hybrid A*, frontier-driven exploration, and encounter-based map fusion so robots reconcile their maps whenever they meet.

MAPFPIBThybrid A*map fusion

Learning for robust flight

Continual learning for detecting and avoiding aerodynamic disturbances in flight, so a vehicle keeps adapting to conditions it never saw during training instead of failing on them.

Selected projects

AirStack — automated batch missions

Added a batch mission system to AirLab's open-source autonomy stack, letting researchers queue and run mission sets on OSMO without babysitting each trial.

Post-earthquake triage simulation

A collapsed-urban environment in Isaac Sim for testing aerial triage and victim search, built from city asset packs and scanned rubble geometry.

PX4 + Gazebo SITL pipeline

A reproducible Docker pipeline for PX4 v1.15.3 with Gazebo Harmonic, for large outdoor flight experiments before touching real hardware.
